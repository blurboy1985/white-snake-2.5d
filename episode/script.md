# Script: The Monk in the Crab

Target 65 to 75 s, one narrator. Follows the popular Qing opera version of the legend. See `sources.md` for variants.

In 1924, a pagoda in Hangzhou fell down. And China's most famous writer cheered. Because a woman was trapped beneath it. A woman who was really a snake.

Her name was Bai Suzhen. A white snake spirit who spent a thousand years learning to be human. On a rainy day on West Lake, a young man named Xu Xian lent her his umbrella. They fell in love. They married, and opened a medicine shop.

But a monk named Fahai knew what she was. At the Dragon Boat Festival, he had Xu Xian give her realgar wine. She drank it, and became a giant white snake. Xu Xian died of fright.

So she flew to Mount Kunlun and stole a herb that wakes the dead. He woke up.

Fahai locked Xu Xian in his temple. She flooded it to get him back. So Fahai trapped her under Leifeng Pagoda. Forever.

Until 1924.

And Fahai? The writer had an answer. Open a crab today. That little monk inside the shell? That's Fahai. Still hiding.
