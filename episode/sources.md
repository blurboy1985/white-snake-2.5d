# Sources

Each claim in `script.md`, the source, and what it says. Checked 2026-10-31.

## Historical facts

| Claim | Source | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leifeng Pagoda in Hangzhou fell in 1924 | [Leifeng Pagoda, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leifeng_Pagoda) | Collapsed the afternoon of 25 September 1924, from disrepair and brick theft. Rebuilt and reopened 25 October 2002. |
| A famous writer cheered the collapse | [Lu Xun, "On the Fall of the Leifeng Pagoda", Wikisource](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/論雷峰塔的倒掉) | Dated 28 October 1924. Lu Xun says he had long hoped it would fall and ends on "活該" (served him right). "Most famous" is our framing of Lu Xun's standing. |
| The "monk inside the crab" is Fahai | Same Lu Xun essay | Lu Xun describes the cone-shaped membrane inside a cooked crab shell, which children called the "crab monk" (蟹和尚), as Fahai hiding from punishment. Folklore, told as folklore. |

## The legend (told as legend, popular Qing opera version)

Source for all rows: [Legend of the White Snake, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legend_of_the_White_Snake).

| Story beat | Note on variants |
| --- | --- |
| Bai Suzhen, a white snake spirit, cultivates for a thousand years to become human | Length of cultivation varies by telling. |
| Meets Xu Xian on West Lake in the rain, he lends her his umbrella | Popular version: on a ferry on West Lake. Feng Menglong's 1624 version also has the rain and the umbrella. The Broken Bridge is where they reunite later in the opera version, so the script does not name it for the meeting. |
| They marry and open a medicine shop | Popular version. |
| Fahai has her drink realgar wine at the Dragon Boat Festival, she shows her snake form, Xu Xian dies of shock | Popular version. Not in the 1624 version. |
| She steals the reviving herb from Mount Kunlun | Added in Fang Chengpei's 1771 play. Some tellings say Mount Emei. |
| Fahai holds Xu Xian at Jinshan Temple, she floods it | Popular version. The flood is much smaller in the 1624 version. |
| Fahai traps her under Leifeng Pagoda | All major versions. |

Left out for length: Xiaoqing (the green snake companion) and the son who later frees his mother. Both are in the popular version.
